# Pitch script

What Eric says on each slide: about 3 minutes at a calm pace (about 140 words a minute).
Don't read it word for word. Each slide's headline is on screen; say the thing that
isn't. Times are rough targets.

## 1 · Title (5 s)

"Hi, we're D3V D3M0Ns. This is ToughCrowd."

## 2 · The problem (25 s)

"In grade 9, Darren and I went to our first case competition. Our idea was good. But we
rushed the pitch, said 'um' every other sentence, and ran out of time. Then a judge
asked [the real question], and we both froze. We'd practised the slides a dozen times.
We'd never practised the questions."

## 3 · Where pitches break (15 s)

"That's where pitches really break: running over time, and freezing on the question. A
timer can tell you that you ran over. It can't tell you why, and it can't ask you the
question you're dreading."

(Optional opening line instead, from `pitch.md`: "Last year's winner prepped you for a job
interview about yourself. ToughCrowd preps you for the panel that's about to tear apart
your project." If you use it, put it right after the title.)

## 4 · The solution (20 s)

"So we built a flight simulator for your pitch. You give it your project: your slides,
or just explain it out loud. A panel of judges questions you, out loud. Our four, or one
you describe, like your actual professor. From friendly to brutal, for a hackathon or a
thesis defense. Then it shows you what you said, and how you said it."

## 5 · Live demo (about 90 s)

"Let's do it on this exact deck."

- Upload the deck (or have it uploaded already, to save time).
- Turn on **Judges interrupt**. Pick one judge (the Engineer is a good one).
- The judge asks out loud. Start answering, and ramble on purpose until they cut in.
  Let the room laugh. Wrap up in the 15 seconds.
- Show the feedback: the score, the four checks, the "um"s in red and the pauses.

Say while it loads: "The judge has read every slide, including the pictures."

## 6 · How it works (25 s)

"How it works. Gemini reads each slide once, pictures included. Claude plays the judges
and grades your answers. ElevenLabs gives every judge a voice, and turns your answer
into text with a timestamp on every word. From those timestamps, our own code counts the
'um's, your pace and every pause. The AI never guesses those numbers."

## 7 · Why it's new (15 s)

"Tools like PowerPoint's Speaker Coach coach how you talk. ToughCrowd also questions you
on your own project, for any panel: hackathons, classes, defenses, demo days."

## 8 · What's next (10 s)

"Next: bring your event's real rubric, team mode where each question goes to whoever built
that part, and tracking which questions still trip you up."

## 9 · Thanks (5 s)

"Thanks for being our tough crowd. Ask us anything."

---

If you're running long, cut slide 3 to one sentence and skip slide 8's details: just say
"rubrics, team mode and tracking."
